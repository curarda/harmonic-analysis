Harmonic Analysis

melody + chords · MIDI
vertical & horizontal · global & local
Give it two MIDI files — a melody and its chords. It lines them up in time and works out what the music is doing: functional harmony, the chord progression, key / mode and where they change, and where the melody sits against the chords and the key — vertically (note-against-chord) and horizontally (motion, intervals). No opinions, no advice — description only.

IVChord progression & functional harmony

Read straight from the chords MIDI — chord name, roman numeral in the local key, and function (T = tonic / home · S = subdominant / away · D = dominant / tension). Chords outside the key are flagged in plum (borrowed · secondary dominant · Neapolitan). Cadences sit between the chords.

VKey, mode & changes — global + local

VIVertical — melody against the chords

Each melody note is classified against the chord sounding under it: is it a chord tone (root / 3rd / 5th / 7th), or a non-chord tone (passing, neighbor, suspension, appoggiatura, anticipation, pedal)? Dissonances on strong beats and tendency-tone resolutions are pulled out too.

VIIHorizontal — melodic profile & intervals

Interval distribution (semitones, signed)
0 = repeated · negative = down · positive = up. Mostly steps (±1–2) = smooth; leaps (≥±5) = motion / hooks.

VIIIEnergy & shape — the melody over time

pitch height note density energy peak

Key by Krumhansl–Schmuckler profile correlation (global, and local via a sliding window). Chords by a time-windowed pitch-class weight vector matched to templates — it catches block chords and arpeggios alike. Roman numerals and function are read in the local key. Vertical classification is chord-tone membership plus context-based non-chord-tone heuristics. Everything runs in the browser; nothing is uploaded.
